# Ethics Statement

IdentityDNA Protocol deals with identity, trust, and access — areas with real consequences for privacy and security. This document sets out the principles guiding the project's development.

## Privacy by Design
The protocol should minimize what it needs to learn about a user in order to verify them. Where possible, verification should rely on cryptographic proofs rather than the collection or storage of raw personal data.

## Transparency Over Marketing Claims
We do not describe this protocol as "unhackable" or "perfectly secure." Security claims are backed by an explicit, published threat model (see `SECURITY.md`), not by marketing language.

## Responsible Use
IdentityDNA Protocol is intended for legitimate authentication use cases: securing accounts, sessions, and access to protected resources. It is not intended to enable covert surveillance, unauthorized tracking, or profiling of individuals without their knowledge or consent.

## Open Review
The specification is published so that researchers, cryptographers, and security practitioners can review, question, and critique it. We consider that scrutiny essential to the protocol's credibility and improvement — not a threat to it.

## Responsible Disclosure
Security researchers who find flaws are encouraged to report them privately first, per `SECURITY.md`, so issues can be addressed before public disclosure.
