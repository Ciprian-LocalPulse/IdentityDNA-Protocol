# Manifesto

## Mission

Redefine digital authentication by replacing static credentials with deterministic identity streams and adaptive cryptographic trust.

## The Problem

Most authentication today still answers one question, once: *"Do you know the password?"* Everything after that moment is assumed. A stolen session token, a hijacked cookie, a compromised device — none of these are caught by a system designed to check identity only at the door.

## The Proposal

IdentityDNA Protocol reframes the question: *"Can you continuously prove that you are the legitimate participant in this session?"*

Identity, under this model, is not a fact verified once and trusted forever. It's a stream — continuously produced, continuously evaluated, and cryptographically bound to the session it belongs to.

## Principles

1. **Continuous over static.** Authentication should not stop at login.
2. **Adaptive over binary.** Trust is a score shaped by context and risk, not a single pass/fail gate.
3. **Ephemeral over reusable.** A session's identity — its Session DNA — should exist only for that session, never replayable elsewhere.
4. **Verifiable over opaque.** The protocol's assumptions, limitations, and threat model should be published and open to scrutiny — not hidden behind marketing claims.
5. **Evolving over finished.** This is a v0.1 Alpha proposal. It is meant to be challenged, tested, and improved by people who understand authentication and cryptography better than any single author does alone.

## What This Is Not

This is not a claim of an unbreakable system. It is a proposal for a different way of thinking about identity in a session — one we believe is worth building, testing, and arguing about in the open.
